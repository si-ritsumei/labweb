Virtual Reality

This study investigates how voice transformation through voice conversion in the metaverse influences speakers’ negotiation behavior.

Multilingual Voice Communication Support in the Metaverse

It is well known that the appearance and movement of the body can affect one’s thoughts and behavior. In the metaverse, the body is represented as an avatar, and changes in the avatar’s appearance can lead to behavior that matches its visual traits. This phenomenon is called the "Proteus effect." For instance, it has been reported that using a taller avatar tends to result in more assertive behavior in negotiation tasks. In this study, we construct a multilingual voice communication environment where users' speech is translated into other languages and converted into synthetic voices, and we examine how these linguistic transformations affect negotiation behavior. The findings are expected to inform the design of user experiences in the metaverse and the development of voice interfaces for intercultural communication.

Presentation Info: Tomoaki Iwano, Yohei Murakami. The Effect of Language Transformation on Speaker Behavior in the Metaverse, IPSJ Annual Conference, 2025.

Analyzing the Impact of Avatar Voice on User Behavior in the Metaverse

Previous studies on the Proteus effect have mainly focused on the visual characteristics of avatars, while the impact of non-visual attributes such as voice and vocal gender on user behavior has not been thoroughly examined. While the preceding study explored voice translation for communication support in the metaverse, this study focuses on the psychological effects of avatar speech on the user’s own behavior. Specifically, we examine how using a voice of the opposite gender in a VR environment affects self-disclosure and negotiation behavior. Participants operate first-person avatars in VRChat and interact with a female experimenter while using a voice changer. We conduct a self-disclosure task ("Two Truths and a Lie") and an ultimatum game to analyze behavioral differences depending on the presence or absence of the voice changer. Additionally, we evaluate participants’ sense of vocal ownership and agency through pre- and post-experiment questionnaires. This study aims to clarify how auditory self-representation in virtual environments affects user behavior, contributing to the design of interaction and self-expression support technologies in future metaverse applications.

Presentation Info: Yuria Mori, Yohei Murakami. The Impact of Voice Changers on Speaker Behavior, IPSJ Annual Conference, 2025.
