著者:Ryo Yamauchi, Yohei Murakami
タイトル: A SharedPlans-Based Open Collaboration Protocol for LLM Multi-Agent Systems
概要:LLM multi-agent systems, in which multiple agents are assigned roles and solve problems by dividing the work, have attracted growing attention. However, most existing studies have not made explicit to what degree agents must share and align their goals, subtasks, responsibilities, capabilities, and expectations of one another before collaborative action can begin. We therefore focus on SharedPlans, a formal theory of collaborative action, and externalize the shared plan state it defines, namely the SP state, as a PlanDoc schema that LLM agents can reference and update. We further propose SharedPlans-Based Open Collaboration Protocol, in which specialist agents collaboratively construct a plan through consensus formation based on their statements of acceptance, expectations, and feasibility on the PlanDoc, and then execute their assigned actions while maintaining the established PlanDoc. To evaluate the proposed method, we use cross-domain QA from MuSiQue as a collaborative task and compare four conditions along two axes: whether agents participate in plan construction and whether the plan is shared during execution. The four conditions are Centralized Planner, Manager-Router, Distributed Planner, and the proposed method. The results show that the proposed method is the only distributed condition that simultaneously establishes the SP-state components—agreement, owned responsibility, mutual belief, and ability belief—and it attains the highest sub-action relevance (alignment between subactions and execution results) and the highest accuracy among the four conditions. Its plan quality is slightly below the Centralized Planner but above the Distributed Planner, and its no-answer rate is the lowest of the four conditions. The contribution of this paper is to operationalize a theoretically defined shared plan state as a PlanDoc schema and SharedPlans-Based Open Collaboration Protocol, and to empirically demonstrate how the degree of SP-state establishment relates to the quality of collaborative behavior.
